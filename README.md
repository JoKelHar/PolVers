# PolVers — a product discovery case study

*Turning a raw idea into a validated, buildable product concept — end to end.*

**Status:** Concept, brand, and validation assets complete · demand-validation in progress · full product spec kept private.

---

## What this is

This repository is a **case study**, not a finished company. During a break between roles I took a single throwaway idea and ran it through the full early-stage product process — problem framing, competitive and failure-mode research, naming and trademark diligence, monetisation design, GDPR-aware data modelling, and a complete brand and motion system — to demonstrate how I think, decide, and ship.

It is deliberately weighted toward **judgment**: the decisions made, the things deliberately killed, and the reasoning behind both.

## The idea

**PolVers** (from *pollice verso*, the turned thumb by which a Roman crowd rendered its verdict) is a proving ground for ideas. You post an idea; a crowd turns its thumb; and it earns a portable verdict — so the people with ideas but no network, and the people with skills but no direction, can find each other and find out what's real before anyone bets a year on it.

The founding insight is uncomfortable and load-bearing: **ideas are cheap; proof is the whole game.** Most startups die building something nobody wanted, largely because validation feels like procrastination next to the dopamine of building. PolVers exists to make that validation the fun part — and, fittingly, this project holds itself to the same rule: the current priority is proving demand, not building the app.

## Process & decisions (the interesting part)

- **Named it properly, and proved it was ownable.** Worked through a series of candidate names, killing each against real constraints — existing software products, trademark collisions in the relevant classes, domain availability, and international pronounceability — before landing on one that cleared all four. Visible in the trail: several strong-but-doomed candidates discarded on evidence rather than taste.
- **Designed the signal to be credible, then caught my own flaw.** An early version let paid users' votes carry more weight — which would have quietly gutted the product's egalitarian premise. Scrapped it. The resolved model separates a democratic mass signal from an expertise-weighted one, where weight is *earned*, never bought.
- **Monetised without selling the un-sellable.** Ideas aren't ownable, so the model never sells "the idea" or false exclusivity; revenue attaches to access, connection, and validated deal-flow instead — which also keeps it clear of the obvious legal traps.
- **Designed for durability.** Made the structural calls early that are cheap now and brutal to retrofit — one shared taxonomy across tagging/expertise/filtering, people and organisations as separate entities, a fixed minimal card with depth pushed to a discussion layer.
- **Built the brand as the product.** A commodity concept lives or dies on its design language, so the work includes a full identity — a single motif, a considered palette, bold typography, and a motion/"sensory" system built on the principle that motion is only ever earned by a real event (dopamine with taste, not a slot machine).
- **Ran a due-diligence pass** against documented startup failure modes and the original playbooks of comparable products, and wrote the honest residual risks down rather than around them.
